Art Recall and The mosque of rome

Art Recall

This week was our first week working on our capstone projects. There are several different projects across the cohort, and all of them have really interesting ideas. Marianne and I started by laying out our core goals and objectives for our project, Art Recall. We defined the main problem we are solving, identified our target audience, discussed what would count as success and how to measure it, and mapped out which new features we can realistically add to our prototype. Our current prototype was mostly focused on standard multiple-choice questions and answers. We wanted to make the experience more playful and enjoyable, so we brainstormed different question formats and came up with four new types:

  • Asking visitors which of two artworks is older.
  • Asking which artwork was made in a specific year.
  • Asking which artwork was not made in a given year.
  • Traditional multiple-choice questions.

We realized that the title and medium categories were far too challenging, even for art specialists, making them nearly impossible for general museum visitors. Because of that, we decided to drop those categories to keep the game fun and accessible. Since we introduced new year-based question formats, we needed a fresh set of artwork spanning different eras. We selected a new batch of artwork images, but because they were too large for our screen, they needed to be resized. Luckily, our existing codebase already included an image-resizing script from our previous 4-category version, which made scaling the new batch quick.

The mosque of rome

We visited a new exhibition, though it turned out to be much shorter than I expected. It mainly featured one main screen playing a documentary video about the history and construction of the Mosque of Rome. However, the other section of the exhibition offered a VR experience, which played out more like an interactive game. It takes you on a guided virtual walkthrough of the mosque as if you were physically standing there, allowing you to examine the architectural details up close. An in-game guide explains the history of the mosque, its design philosophy, and what its architectural elements represent. I loved the VR experience, it was super fun and engaging. It showed me how effective virtual reality can be for introducing people to real-world places, building curiosity, and motivating visitors to research the site or even planning a physical trip to visit it in person.
